\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Holding the conditioner constant, the NCA's iterated-local-update body beats non-iterative CNN, U-Net, and ViT baselines on autoregressive rollout across every preset (Table~\ref{tab:baselines-arch}); within the NCA family the shared-weight body matches the per-step body at $\pm 1\sigma$ on \textsc{Train-14} with notably fewer parameters and lower seed variance.
A single-grid win does not, however, establish learned dynamics, since latent cross-attention can in principle memorize per-state outcomes; \textsc{Sokoban-Evolved} (one rule set across 1024 levels) removes that crutch and sets a ceiling on what the recipe extracts from the rule alone.

Within the recipe, axis-cummax contributes surprisingly on Sokoban given that its push rule only references a $3{\times}3$ neighborhood, though the seed std on that ablation row roughly matches the gap so part of the effect is plausibly noise; cleaner gains are expected on long-range patterns like \textit{neko puzzle}'s ellipsis-spanning rule.
Input-skip cuts the other way: re-injecting $(s_t, a_t)$ at every step anchors the body to its input, trading per-step fit for AR robustness when the rollout feeds back slightly-off predictions.

Rule-conditioning amortizes shared dynamics across games: in-distribution it slows per-game capacity dilution by roughly an order of magnitude on the 14-game intersection against a parameter-matched unconditional baseline ($n_h{=}288$ uncond vs $n_h{=}256$ cond, $\approx 16.6$M vs $16.0$M total params).
On unseen games the cond/uncond ordering shifts across scales: tied at \textsc{Train-14}, cond clearly wins at \textsc{Train-59}, parameter-matched uncond slightly surpasses cond on wins-vs-identity at \textsc{Train-199}.
This pulls apart two effects: an in-distribution role where the encoder is clearly load-bearing ($\approx 6\times$ lower 1-step (TF) cell-error than uncond at \textsc{Train-199}), and an out-of-distribution role where its slot representations do not transfer to unseen rule sets at the corpus sizes tested.
With no rule channel the unconditional model only consumes state and action grids, so any OOD gain must come from level-layout features---object identities, wall topology, win-condition cues---which at \textsc{Train-199} alone match cond on \textsc{Heldout-26}.
Whether further rule diversity (Train-500 / -1000) triggers a phase transition---in the spirit of grokking on algorithmic tasks~\cite{power2022grokking,nanda2023progress}---after which the encoder's slot space becomes the load-bearing OOD signal, remains open.
