\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Holding the conditioner constant, the NCA's iterated-local-update body
beats every non-iterative baseline (CNN, U-Net, ViT) on autoregressive
rollout across every preset we tested (Table~\ref{tab:baselines-arch}).
Within the NCA family, the shared-weight body matches the per-step
body's mean BFS rollout error at $\pm 1\sigma$ on \textsc{Train-14}
with $3.8\times$ fewer parameters and $\approx 5.8\times$ lower
seed-to-seed variance.
Rule-conditioning amortises shared dynamics across games: on the
14-game intersection, conditional 1-step (TF) error grows $\approx
5\times$ from \textsc{Train-14} to \textsc{Train-59} while
unconditional grows $\approx 25\times$ (Appendix~\ref{app:dilution});
on unseen games, scaling to \textsc{Train-199} closes most of the gap
to the identity baseline
(\S\ref{sec:results-encoder-ablation}). Inverse-fitting from observed
transitions recovers OOD dynamics through the latent variable to
$\approx 10^{-3}$ BCE (Appendix~\ref{app:inverse-fit}). Together these
results support the first half of the engine-modeling agenda; a full
claim additionally requires smoother scaling with the number of games,
held-out rule-system generalization, and inverse-fitting from
transitions.

The unconditional model's scale-driven OOD improvement suggests it
is learning to infer game mechanics from level layouts. The encoder's
clean OOD advantage holds only at \textsc{Train-14}; at
\textsc{Train-59} and \textsc{Train-199} the unconditional baseline
catches up and on some summaries overtakes it
(\S\ref{sec:results-encoder-ablation},
Figure~\ref{fig:intersection-slope-ood}). With no rule encoding the
unconditional model only consumes state and action grids, so any gain
on unseen games must come from features it extracts from the level
layout itself---object identities and counts, wall topology,
win-condition cues, character positions. Scaling beyond
\textsc{Train-14} therefore appears to induce an implicit ability to infer mechanics
from layout, making the explicit rule channel partially redundant on
unseen games once the corpus is wide enough. This is consistent with
our deliberately level-independent slot encoder leaving layout
information on the table; perceiver-IO-style attention from slots
back into the grid is a natural way for a conditional model to
combine the explicit rule channel with the grid-derived signal that
the unconditional baseline already exploits implicitly. The encoder's
load-bearing claim, then, is in-distribution: it slows per-game
capacity dilution by an order of magnitude on the shared 14-game
intersection, where data dilution---not rule disambiguation---is the
binding constraint.
An open question is whether further data and training would eventually trigger a phase transition---in the spirit of grokking on algorithmic
tasks~\cite{power2022grokking,nanda2023progress}---in which the rule
channel becomes load-bearing: the model would shift from inferring
mechanics from layout statistics to interpreting the rule text
directly, and OOD performance would then track the encoder rather
than the unconditional baseline.

A single-grid win does not establish that the model has learned the
dynamics, because slot cross-attention can in principle memorize
per-state outcomes (\textsc{Sokoban-Authored} block,
Table~\ref{tab:baselines-arch}). Once that crutch is removed---one
rule set across 1024 levels at four widths---the same shared-NCA
architecture climbs from $0.11\%$ BFS final-error on authored to
$4.37\%$ on \textsc{Sokoban-Evolved} (same table), $\approx
40\times$ higher and setting a ceiling on what the recipe extracts
from the rule alone. Wider grids, capacity scans, and adaptive-halting
probes are in Appendix~\ref{app:ceiling}. We propose this preset as a
default check for future PuzzleScript world-model work: if a model
only succeeds at the single-grid setting, it may not be learning the
dynamics.

Inverse-fitting (Appendix~\ref{app:inverse-fit}) opens a ``latent
world coder'' direction in which the learned dynamics manifold
becomes a search space for novel rule systems, with successful
hypotheses projected back into executable PuzzleScript through the
symbolic decoder (Appendix~\ref{app:autoencoder}). This complements
program-synthesis approaches to world modeling and symbolic-library
induction~\cite{tang2024worldcoder,ahmed2025theorycoder,ellis2021dreamcoder};
pairing inverse-fit slots with engine-loadable reconstructions
remains an open thread. Interactive benchmarks such as
AutumnBench~\cite{warrier2025benchmarking} and
ARC-AGI-3~\cite{arcprize2026arcagi3} are natural downstream targets,
since rule inference from limited interaction benefits directly from
a fast forward model. The architecture is not PuzzleScript-specific:
any deterministic discrete-grid simulator with declarative rules
(Conway's Game of Life, lattice-gas CAs, reaction-diffusion CAs)
admits the same pattern, and we expect the non-iterative gap to
widen as the engine's outer-loop iteration count grows.

Two design choices bound what we report. Our slot encoder is
intentionally level-independent---slots depend only on rule tokens,
not on the level's spatial state---so one encoded game corresponds to
many levels but slot capacity is capped; perceiver-IO-style attention
back into the grid is a natural extension. We also restrict to
single-step prediction; multi-step training ($s_{t+k}$ for $k > 1$)
is straightforward but unexplored.
