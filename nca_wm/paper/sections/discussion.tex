\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Holding the conditioner constant, the NCA's iterated-local-update body beats non-iterative CNN, U-Net, and ViT baselines on autoregressive rollout across every preset (Table~\ref{tab:baselines-arch}); within the NCA family the shared-weight body matches the per-step body at $\pm 1\sigma$ on \textsc{Train-14} with $3.8\times$ fewer parameters and $\approx 5.8\times$ lower seed variance.
Rule-conditioning amortises shared dynamics across games: on the 14-game intersection conditional 1-step (TF) error grows $\approx 5\times$ from \textsc{Train-14} to \textsc{Train-59} while unconditional grows $\approx 25\times$ (Appendix~\ref{app:dilution}); on unseen games, scaling to \textsc{Train-199} closes most of the gap to identity (\S\ref{sec:results-encoder-ablation}).

The encoder's clean OOD advantage holds only at \textsc{Train-14}; at \textsc{Train-59} and \textsc{Train-199} the unconditional baseline catches up and on some summaries overtakes it.
With no rule channel the unconditional model only consumes state and action grids, so any OOD gain must come from level-layout features---object identities, wall topology, win-condition cues---that scaling beyond \textsc{Train-14} appears to make extractable from layout alone.
The encoder's load-bearing claim is in-distribution, where it slows per-game capacity dilution by an order of magnitude.
Whether further data and training would trigger a phase transition---in the spirit of grokking on algorithmic tasks~\cite{power2022grokking,nanda2023progress}---after which the rule channel becomes load-bearing and OOD tracks the encoder rather than the unconditional baseline, is an open question.

A single-grid win does not establish learned dynamics, because slot cross-attention can in principle memorize per-state outcomes (\textsc{Sokoban-Authored} block, Table~\ref{tab:baselines-arch}).
Once that crutch is removed---1024 levels at four widths sharing one rule set---the same shared-NCA architecture climbs from $0.11\%$ to $4.37\%$ BFS final-error on \textsc{Sokoban-Evolved} ($\approx 40\times$ higher), setting a ceiling on what the recipe extracts from the rule alone.
We propose this preset as a default check for future PuzzleScript world-model work.
