\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Holding the conditioner constant, the NCA's iterated-local-update
body beats every non-iterative baseline (CNN, U-Net, ViT) on
autoregressive rollout, on every preset we tested
(Tables~\ref{tab:baselines-sokoban}, \ref{tab:baselines-synth},
\ref{tab:baselines-scaling14}). Within the NCA family, the
shared-weight body matches the per-step body's mean BFS rollout
error at $\pm 1\sigma$ on \textsc{Train-14} with $3.8\times$ fewer
parameters and $\approx 5.8\times$ lower seed-to-seed variance
(Table~\ref{tab:baselines-scaling14}). In-distribution rollout
fidelity stays strong as the corpus grows (median 1-step
teacher-forced cell-error of $0.01\%$ at \textsc{Train-14},
$0.10\%$ at \textsc{Train-59}, and $0.45\%$ at \textsc{Train-199};
Table~\ref{tab:indist-scaling}), and on unseen games broad-corpus
training closes most of the gap to the identity baseline at
\textsc{Train-199} (\S\ref{sec:results-encoder-ablation}). Inverse
fitting from observed transitions further recovers OOD games'
dynamics through the latent variable to $\approx 10^{-3}$ BCE
(Appendix~\ref{app:inverse-fit}). Together these results support the
first half of moving from modeling one game to modeling a space of
games organized by dynamics: a rule-conditioned, iterated
local-update model is a strong forward model for PuzzleScript-style
grid dynamics and is more parameter-efficient than several
non-iterative baselines. A full engine-model claim additionally
requires smooth scaling with the number of games, held-out
rule-system generalization, and inverse fitting from transitions.

\paragraph{The memorization confound and the multi-grid ceiling.}
The single-level / single-grid setting
(Table~\ref{tab:baselines-sokoban}) leaves room for the slot
cross-attention to learn a per-state lookup table, so a single-grid
win does not by itself establish that the model has learned the
dynamics. Once that crutch is removed---one rule set shared across
$1024$ levels at four widths---the same shared-NCA architecture
climbs from $0.11\%$ BFS final-error on the authored set to
$4.37\%$ on the multi-grid synthetic set
(Table~\ref{tab:baselines-synth}), $\approx 40\times$ higher,
setting a ceiling on what the present recipe extracts from the rule
alone. Wider grids, capacity scans, and adaptive-halting probes of
this gap are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:ceiling}; mechanism
attribution is left to future work. We treat the multi-grid synth
preset as a default check for future PuzzleScript world-model work:
if a model only succeeds at the single-grid setting, it may not be
learning the dynamics. Breaking this ceiling is the remaining gap
in the engine-modeling story.

\paragraph{Future work and limitations.}
Inverse fitting from observed transitions
(Appendix~\ref{app:inverse-fit}) points toward a ``latent world
coder'' direction: treat a learned dynamics manifold as the search
space for novel rule systems, and project successful latent
hypotheses back into executable symbolic programs through the
mapping reported separately in Appendix~\ref{app:autoencoder}. This
is complementary to program-synthesis world-model agents such as
WorldCoder and TheoryCoder, which synthesize Python transition
models from
interaction~\cite{tang2024worldcoder,ahmed2025theorycoder}, and to
DreamCoder-style systems that grow reusable symbolic libraries for
program induction~\cite{ellis2021dreamcoder}. Pairing inverse-fit
slots with engine-loadable program reconstructions
(Appendix~\ref{app:autoencoder}) is still an open thread that we
report as an early exploratory signal. Interactive world-model
benchmarks such as AutumnBench~\cite{warrier2025benchmarking} and
ARC-AGI-3~\cite{arcprize2026arcagi3} are natural targets, because
acting in novel rule systems after limited interaction benefits
directly from inferring a fast forward model that downstream search
or RL can plan against. The architecture itself is not
domain-specific: any deterministic discrete-grid simulator with
declarative rules (Conway's Game of Life and its variants,
cellular-automata gas dynamics, Asari-style chemical-reaction
grids) admits the same modeling pattern, and we expect the
comparison against non-iterative baselines to favor the NCA more
strongly as the underlying engine's outer-loop iteration count
grows. On the limitations side, our slot encoder is
level-independent: the slots depend only on the game's rule tokens,
not on the level's spatial state. This is the intended design (one
encoded game $\Rightarrow$ many levels) but it caps what the slots
can carry; a version where slots can attend back into the input
grid (perceiver-IO style) would be a natural extension. We also
restrict to single-step prediction; multi-step training (predict
$s_{t+k}$ for $k > 1$) is straightforward to add and not yet
explored.
