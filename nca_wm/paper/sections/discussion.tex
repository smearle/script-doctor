\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Holding the conditioner constant, the NCA's iterated-local-update body
beats every non-iterative baseline (CNN, U-Net, ViT) on autoregressive
rollout across every preset we tested (Tables~\ref{tab:baselines-sokoban},
\ref{tab:baselines-synth}, \ref{tab:baselines-scaling14}). Within the
NCA family, the shared-weight body matches the per-step body's mean BFS
rollout error at $\pm 1\sigma$ on \textsc{Train-14} with $3.8\times$
fewer parameters and $\approx 5.8\times$ lower seed-to-seed variance.
Rule-conditioning amortises shared dynamics across games: on the
14-game intersection, conditional 1-step (TF) error grows $\approx
5\times$ from \textsc{Train-14} to \textsc{Train-59} while
unconditional grows $\approx 25\times$ (Appendix~\ref{app:dilution});
on unseen games, broad-corpus training closes most of the gap to the
identity baseline at \textsc{Train-199}
(\S\ref{sec:results-encoder-ablation}). Inverse-fitting from observed
transitions recovers OOD dynamics through the latent variable to
$\approx 10^{-3}$ BCE (Appendix~\ref{app:inverse-fit}). Together these
results support the first half of the engine-modeling agenda; a full
claim additionally requires smoother scaling with the number of games,
held-out rule-system generalization, and inverse-fitting from
transitions.

\paragraph{The memorization confound and the multi-grid ceiling.}
The single-level/single-grid setting (Table~\ref{tab:baselines-sokoban})
leaves room for the slot cross-attention to learn a per-state lookup, so
a single-grid win does not establish that the model learned the
dynamics. Once that crutch is removed---one rule set across 1024 levels
at four widths---the same shared-NCA architecture climbs from $0.11\%$
BFS final-error on authored to $4.37\%$ on multi-grid synth
(Table~\ref{tab:baselines-synth}), $\approx 40\times$ higher and setting
a ceiling on what the recipe extracts from the rule alone. Wider grids,
capacity scans, and adaptive-halting probes are in
Appendix~\ref{app:ceiling}. We propose this preset as a default check
for future PuzzleScript world-model work: if a model only succeeds at
the single-grid setting, it may not be learning the dynamics.

\paragraph{Future work and limitations.}
Inverse-fitting (Appendix~\ref{app:inverse-fit}) points toward a
``latent world coder'' direction: search a learned dynamics manifold
for novel rule systems and project successful hypotheses back into
executable PuzzleScript through the mapping in
Appendix~\ref{app:autoencoder}. This complements program-synthesis approaches to world modeling and symbolic-library induction~\cite{tang2024worldcoder,ahmed2025theorycoder,ellis2021dreamcoder}; pairing inverse-fit slots with engine-loadable reconstructions remains an open thread.
Interactive benchmarks such as
AutumnBench~\cite{warrier2025benchmarking} and
ARC-AGI-3~\cite{arcprize2026arcagi3} are natural downstream targets,
since rule inference from limited interaction benefits directly from a
fast forward model. The architecture is not PuzzleScript-specific:
any deterministic discrete-grid simulator with declarative rules
(Conway's Game of Life, lattice-gas CAs, reaction-diffusion CAs)
admits the same pattern, and we expect the non-iterative gap to widen
as the engine's outer-loop iteration count grows.

\emph{Limitations.} Our encoder is level-independent: slots depend
only on rule tokens, not on the level's spatial state. This is
intentional (one encoded game $\Rightarrow$ many levels) but caps
slot capacity; perceiver-IO-style attention back into the grid is a
natural extension. We also restrict to single-step prediction;
multi-step training ($s_{t+k}$ for $k > 1$) is straightforward but
unexplored.
