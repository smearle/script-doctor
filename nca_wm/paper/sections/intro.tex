Learned world models are typically trained on pixel
observations~\cite{ha2018world,kaiser2020simple,hafner2019planet,hafner2021dreamerv2,hafner2023dreamerv3,wu2022daydreamer},
with a high-capacity convolutional, U-Net, or video Transformer body
paired to an action-conditioned dynamics
module~\cite{micheli2022iris,robine2023transformer,valevski2025gamengen,alonso2024diamond,bruce2024genie,yang2024unisim,parkerholder2025genie3,savva2026solaris}.
This is a natural fit for visually rich, mechanically uniform
environments, but a less natural one for games whose difficulty lies
in a large space of symbolic rule systems rather than in rendering.

PuzzleScript~\cite{lavelle2013puzzlescript} games are such a regime.
Each game is specified by a short text file of object kinds, layered grid levels, and declarative rewrite rules, e.g.\ \texttt{[ > Player | Crate ] -> [ > Player | > Crate ]}.
The engine evaluates the rule list in order at every tick, repeating the block while \texttt{again} fires.
State is a discrete multi-channel grid, dynamics are deterministic and combinatorial, and the per-tick computation is a sequence of atomic local updates.
PuzzleScript source admits static and tutorial-trace analysis~\cite{julia2023scriptbutler,vet2024puzzle}, and {PuzzleJAX}~\cite{earle2025puzzlejax} reimplements the engine in JAX for large-scale learning.

We propose a \emph{rule-conditioned Neural Cellular Automaton} (NCA)~\cite{mordvintsev2020growing}
world model. A perceiver-style encoder maps the tokenized game spec to
$K$ latent slots; the dynamics body is a local update rule applied for
$T$ iterations on a hidden grid, cross-attending to those slots at
every step. Sharing body parameters across iterations mirrors the
engine's outer loop; the conditioner makes the dynamics portable across
games. Predicting the engine state directly (rather than pixels) lets
us measure fidelity cell-by-cell against the engine.

We train on up to 199 token-distinct human-authored games with between 1-20 rules each.
For each level of each game, we collect bounded A* transition ($\approx 15$M transitions); held-out evaluation uses \textsc{Heldout-26}, a 26-game stratified sample disjoint from training by token-sequence hash after structural canonicalization.

We find that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{NCA dynamics effectively model discrete state transitions.}
        A shared, iteratively-applied local update body operating on the engine's native discrete grid outperforms non-iterative baselines (CNN, U-Net, ViT) on rollout fidelity.
  \item \textbf{Rule-conditioning allows learning multiple games more effectively.}
        Cross-attending to slot embeddings of each game's tokenized rule text lets a single set of weights cover many games and slows per-game capacity dilution by roughly an order of magnitude as the corpus grows.
  \item \textbf{Models do not generalize well, and rule conditioning does not help.}
        Broadening the training corpus closes most of the 1-step gap to the identity baseline on \textsc{Heldout-26}, but the rule encoder shows little advantage over an unconditional baseline at any training scale---suggesting the model is inferring dynamics from level layouts rather than from the explicit rule channel.
\end{enumerate}
