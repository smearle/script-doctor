\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

World-model research has largely been organised around individual,
visually rich games, spending capacity on pixels rather than the
symbolic mechanics that determine what happens next. We investigate
the complementary regime: a single dynamics model spanning a family of
mechanically distinct, fully discrete environments, light enough to
study at the rule level on commodity hardware.

We propose a rule-conditioned Neural Cellular Automaton world model.
Three design choices work in concert.
\textbf{(i) Iterated local-update body.} A single shared update
applied for several iterations on a hidden grid mirrors the engine's
outer loop and beats non-iterative CNN, U-Net, and ViT baselines on
autoregressive rollout fidelity, both per-game and across many
jointly trained games (\S\ref{sec:results-baselines},
\S\ref{sec:results-scaling}).
\textbf{(ii) Rule conditioning.} Cross-attention to slot embeddings of
each game's rule text lets one set of weights model many games. On the
14 games shared by every training preset
(Figure~\ref{fig:intersection-slope}), per-game in-distribution accuracy
degrades as the corpus widens (data dilution at fixed capacity), but
rule-conditioning slows that dilution by roughly an order of magnitude
versus an unconditional baseline.
\textbf{(iii) Training-data scaling.} Broadening the training corpus
markedly improves out-of-distribution performance on
\textsc{Heldout-30}, closing most of the gap to the identity baseline
and yielding wins on a non-trivial fraction of games
(\S\ref{sec:results-encoder-ablation}). The OOD contribution is driven
primarily by rule diversity in the training corpus rather than the
encoder in isolation.

Downstream applications include sample-efficient policy learning and
gradient-based planning across many environments without per-game
retraining; fast adaptation on interactive world-model benchmarks where
rule inference from limited interaction is the central
challenge~\cite{warrier2025benchmarking,arcprize2026arcagi3}; and a
bridge between neural world models and symbolic program learning via a
latent space that doubles as a search space over executable game
programs (preliminary probes in Appendices~\ref{app:autoencoder}
and~\ref{app:inverse-fit}). We also release the held-out evaluation
protocol (token-hash-deduplicated \textsc{Heldout-30}, identity
baseline, wins-vs-identity counter) as a methodological contribution
for testing rule-system generality of future world models.
