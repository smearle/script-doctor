\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

% \paragraph{World models and self-prediction.}
World-model research traces back to Schmidhuber's curiosity
work~\cite{schmidhuber1990make,schmidhuber2022annotated} and was
popularized for vision-based RL by Ha and
Schmidhuber~\cite{ha2018world}. Transformer-based world
models~\cite{micheli2022iris,robine2023transformer} compress images to
discrete tokens and roll out dynamics autoregressively;
dynamics-centric video work argues that dense appearance prediction
spends capacity on visual detail rather than on the trajectories that
drive scene evolution~\cite{baumann2026envisioning}. Interactive video
world models push toward game-like simulation from
pixels~\cite{valevski2025gamengen,parkerholder2025genie3,savva2026solaris},
with scaffolding for long-horizon consistency via self-rollout and
rolling caches~\cite{huang2025selfforcing}, causal diffusion
schedules~\cite{chen2024diffusionforcing}, and external memory or
retrieval~\cite{xiao2025worldmem,oshima2025worldpack}. These methods
encourage latent state to persist despite dense visual prediction; we
take a more direct route by predicting the engine state itself,
conditioned on the program defining the dynamics. Our domain---fully
discrete grid observations with authored rule sets shared across
games---is closer to procedurally-generated grid
benchmarks~\cite{kuettler2020nethack,samvelyan2021minihack,hafner2021crafter,matthews2024craftax}
than to pixel-based Atari~\cite{bellemare2013arcade}. Unlike MuZero-style
models~\cite{schrittwieser2020muzero}, which learn abstract latent
dynamics, we commit to predicting the engine state in its native grid,
making ground-truth supervision exact and cell-by-cell.

% \paragraph{Neural Cellular Automata.}
NCAs learn a local update rule that, applied repeatedly to a grid of
hidden states, gives rise to globally coordinated
behavior~\cite{mordvintsev2020growing}. Subsequent work covers seeded
morphology growth, classification under distribution shift, texture
synthesis, and self-organizing
attention~\cite{niklasson2021selforganising,palm2022variational,najarro2022hypernca};
3D artefacts and functional machines in
Minecraft~\cite{sudhakaran2021growing}; graph algorithms on grid
mazes~\cite{earle2023pathfinding}; populations of continually-adapting
NCA agents in differentiable ALife
substrates~\cite{berdica2026pbtnca}; and compilation of symbolic
rewrite rules into NCA dynamics for grid-based RL
environments~\cite{earle2025autoverse}. We use NCAs as
action-conditioned world models: rather than asking the NCA to
converge to a static target, we ask it to predict the engine's
deterministic next state at every tick.

% \paragraph{Learned grid-engine and local-update models.}
\cite{bamford2020neural} similarly use an NCA-like 2D recurrent local-update system with shared convolutional GRU updates~\cite{kaiser2016neuralgpu} conditioned on the
player action, trained from pixels with a separate forward model per
GVGAI game. We instead train one rule-conditioned model across many
games, predict the engine's symbolic object-channel state, and
condition on the tokenized game program. ConvLSTMs~\cite{shi2015convlstm},
graph neural networks, and diffusion-style iterative refinement also
use repeated local computation; NCAs differ in committing to a
\emph{shared} (across iterations) update rule on a fixed grid, which
matches the PuzzleScript engine's outer-loop semantics.

% \paragraph{PuzzleScript and rule-based puzzle games.}
PuzzleScript~\cite{lavelle2013puzzlescript} is a DSL for grid-based
puzzle games supporting chained local interactions, \texttt{again}-driven
simulation (gravity, projectiles, light beams), multi-character control,
and rule priorities. Thousands of community games share its tokenization
and state encoding, making the family a natural multi-game benchmark for
world models. PuzzleJax~\cite{earle2025puzzlejax} introduced a
reasoning-and-learning benchmark with a JAX engine and three game
collections: the PuzzleScript Gallery~\cite{puzzlescript_gallery} (curated
by increpare), an Archive~\cite{pedro2019database}, and a long-tail
collection of gists. We draw from the same three collections and add a
C++ engine backend, cached transition datasets, and rule-conditioned
forward models.
