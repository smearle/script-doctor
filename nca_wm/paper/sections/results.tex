\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We organise the results around the three contributions in
\S\ref{sec:intro}. \S\ref{sec:results-baselines} compares the
rule-conditioned NCA body against three non-iterative baselines on
single-game and multi-grid-synthetic suites; \S\ref{sec:results-pool}
decomposes that comparison via a per-feature pool/input-skip ablation;
\S\ref{sec:results-scaling} extends the comparison to a 14-game
multi-game preset. \S\ref{sec:results-fidelity} reports
in-distribution dynamics fidelity on the 94-game broad-corpus preset.
\S\ref{sec:results-ood} reports generalization to unseen games via
held-out latent geometry, symbolic decoder reconstruction / sampling,
and frozen-NCA inverse fitting from OOD transitions. Findings on
within-game OOD levels, the hierarchical weight-sharing knob, the
adaptive-halting head, and the multi-grid synth-data ceiling are
deferred to the appendix.

\subsection{Architecture comparison: NCA vs.\ non-iterative baselines}
\label{sec:results-baselines}

Table~\ref{tab:baselines-microban} reports the head-to-head comparison
on \textsc{microban} (10 authored levels), trained for 10k updates per
seed at $d_h = 256$ with the canonical recipe (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).
We measure best train cross-entropy loss, end-of-rollout cell-error
rate under three policies (random, BFS-optimal, A*-optimal), and final
parameter count.

The NCA with shared weights wins on BFS rollout
($0.11\% \pm 0.16\%$) at the fewest parameters (1.94M),
$2.9\times$ better than the same-depth feed-forward CNN
($0.32\% \pm 0.24\%$) at $2.6\times$ fewer params and
$4.3\times$ better than the per-step NCA ($0.47\% \pm 0.66\%$).
U-Net's 2-level downsampling loses fine-grained player position on
small grids; ViT's global self-attention diverges before the first
rollout step (mean first-divergence step under $1$). Best train loss
is essentially identical ($0.107 \pm 0.002$) across all five
architectures despite the $\geq 100\times$ swing in autoregressive
rollout error; we report both metrics throughout because the per-step
training objective is invisible to compounding rollout drift.

The single-game setting allows the model to memorize per-state
outcomes via cross-attention over slot embeddings. To force the model
to actually learn the rule, Table~\ref{tab:baselines-synth} repeats
the comparison on the multi-grid synthetic regime: four widths
$\{5,6,7,8\}^2$ $\times$ 256 synthetic levels per width, sharing one
\textsc{sokoban\_basic} rule set. Per-state memorization is
unavailable (1024 distinct levels share one rule); the model has to
learn the slide rule across grid sizes.

The shared-weights NCA again wins on BFS rollout
($4.37\% \pm 2.02\%$) at the fewest parameters, with the per-step

subsection; longer-form architecture findings (per-game grid,
hierarchical weight sharing) are in
Appendix~\ref{app:per-game-arch}.

\subsection{Per-feature pool and input-skip ablation}
\label{sec:results-pool}

We decompose the NCA-shared advantage on the multi-grid synth setting
(Table~\ref{tab:baselines-synth}) by individually disabling each
component of the pool stack and the input-skip connection. Every
NCA step in the full recipe sees three flavours of broadcast
non-local features --- axis max-pool (row + column max), axis
cumulative max (directional prefix-max in each of four directions),
and grid-wide global max --- plus a re-injection of the embedded
$(s_t, a_t)$ at every step.

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{\textbf{Per-feature ablation of the NCA-shared recipe on
    multi-grid synth \textsc{sokoban\_basic}.} Mean $\pm$ std over 3
    seeds at 10k updates. $\Delta$BFS reports the absolute increase
    in BFS final cell-error vs.\ the full recipe; relative figures
    in parentheses. Train loss is essentially constant across all
    configurations.}
  \label{tab:ablation-pool}
  \small
  \resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{%
  \begin{tabular}{l c c c c}
    \toprule
    Recipe & params (M) & best loss & BFS final-err & $\Delta$BFS vs.\ full \\
    \midrule
    \textbf{Full (axis + cummax + global + input-skip)} & 3.07 & $0.098 \pm 0.004$ & $\mathbf{4.37\% \pm 2.02\%}$ & --- \\
    \midrule
    no axis-cummax    & 2.94 & $0.098 \pm 0.003$ & $4.37\% \pm 2.24\%$  & $+0.00$ ($+0\%$) \\
    no input-skip     & 2.48 & $0.098 \pm 0.004$ & $5.95\% \pm 4.24\%$  & $+1.58$ ($+36\%$) \\
    no axis-pool      & 2.94 & $0.100 \pm 0.004$ & $6.75\% \pm 0.56\%$  & $+2.38$ ($+55\%$) \\
    no global-pool    & 3.01 & $0.098 \pm 0.005$ & $10.71\% \pm 2.57\%$ & $+6.35$ ($+145\%$) \\
    no pool at all    & 2.68 & $0.100 \pm 0.005$ & $10.71\% \pm 3.89\%$ & $+6.35$ ($+145\%$) \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  }
\end{table}

\emph{Global-pool} is the single most important pool feature: removing
it alone is as bad as removing all three pool flavours combined
($+145\%$ relative BFS error in both cases), indicating that
grid-wide presence-of-X signals are doing all the non-local work on
this task. \emph{Axis-cummax} --- the directional prefix-max designed
for axis-aligned ``rule fires once X is in the same row/column as
Y'' patterns --- contributes nothing measurable on
\textsc{sokoban\_basic}: Sokoban chained-pushing rules look like
\texttt{[ Crate | Crate ] -> [\,| Crate Crate\,]}, which depends on
the immediate $3{\times}3$ neighbourhood, not a directional axial
lookup. We expect the cummax feature to recover its load-bearing role
on games whose rule sets do reference far-apart same-axis cells
(e.g.\ \textsc{collapse}, \textsc{varislide}). \emph{Input-skip}
contributes a modest $+36\%$ but with the highest variance across
configurations ($\sigma = 4.24\%$), suggesting it is a stability
mechanism rather than an information-carrying feature.

Both NCA variants outperform all three non-NCA baselines on
autoregressive rollout, with U-Net at $1.6\times$ and ViT at

($\sim$$8 \times 10^{-4}$ vs.\ $\sim$$0$), confirming the structural
mismatch from the single-game setting. Per-game breakdowns are in

\subsection{In-distribution mastery on the broad corpus}
\label{sec:results-fidelity}

Rule-conditioning lets a single set of weights master many games at
once. Table~\ref{tab:fidelity} reports per-cell rollout error on the
94-game broad-corpus preset \textsc{Train-94}, averaged across all
94 games and all authored levels per game, under four rollout
regimes: random actions (autoregressive),
random-action teacher-forced (one step at a time), BFS-optimal
oracle actions, and A*-optimal oracle actions. We compare the
encoder-only run ($\beta = 0$) and the joint-decoder run
($\beta = 1.0$), where $\beta$ controls the weight of the symbolic
token-decoder loss in the training objective.

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{\textbf{Per-cell rollout error on the 94-game broad-corpus
  training preset.} Mean and median across all 94 games (each game's
  number is the per-rollout-step mean across all authored levels,
  50 steps for autoregressive regimes / level-length for BFS / A*).
  Adding the joint decoder objective is essentially \emph{free} on
  rollout fidelity: every regime ties within 0.5pp.}
  \label{tab:fidelity}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{l c c c c}
    \toprule
    Regime & v3 mean & v3 median & v3+dec mean & v3+dec median \\
    \midrule
    random (AR)        & $7.41\%$ & $2.46\%$ & $8.07\%$ & $2.74\%$ \\
    random\_tf (1-step)& $1.39\%$ & $\mathbf{0.15\%}$ & $1.38\%$ & $\mathbf{0.14\%}$ \\
    BFS (oracle)       & $7.00\%$ & $1.86\%$ & $7.59\%$ & $2.35\%$ \\
    A* (oracle)        & $5.30\%$ & $2.22\%$ & $5.42\%$ & $2.67\%$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The 1-step teacher-forced cell-error of $\sim 0.15\%$ across 94 games
quantifies on-distribution fidelity in a single number: a single
rule-conditioned NCA, fed only the tokenized rule text and the
current state, predicts the next state of any of 94 different
authored games at $\geq 99.85\%$ per-cell accuracy median.
Multi-step rollout error climbs to $\sim 5{-}8\%$ across regimes,
with the median game still under $3\%$ on every regime. The mean is
pulled up by a small set of persistently-hard games whose state
spaces are large or whose rule structures involve cascading
\texttt{again} loops; we discuss this distribution in
\S\ref{sec:discussion}. Adding the joint symbolic-decoder objective
($\beta = 1.0$) ties the encoder-only run within $0.5$pp on every
regime, so the dynamics-fidelity claim is robust to that auxiliary
loss.

\subsection{Generalization to unseen games}
\label{sec:results-ood}

We evaluate generalization to a 30-game stratified held-out set,
disjoint from every training preset by name and by token-sequence
hash, along three complementary axes: held-out latent geometry,
symbolic-decoder reconstruction and sampling, and frozen-NCA
inverse-fitting from observed transitions.

\paragraph{Latent geometry as we scale games.}
\label{sec:results-scaling-curve}
We train the same rule-conditioned NCA architecture on nested
training presets and measure each held-out game's 1-NN cosine
distance to the nearest training-game slot in the 1024-dim flattened
slot space. Table~\ref{tab:scaling-curve} reports the median across
the 21 of 30 held-outs whose tokens fit within the smallest training
preset's maximum sequence length.

\paragraph{Symbolic decoder: reconstruction and sampling.}
\label{sec:results-autoencoder}
The joint encoder + NCA + slot-token decoder
(\S\ref{sec:method}) is trained on \textsc{Train-94} for 150k updates
with $\beta = 1.0$. The decoder converges to per-token
cross-entropy $\approx 2 \times 10^{-9}$ within the first 5k updates
and holds there throughout, adding 1.18M parameters on top of the
9.89M world-model parameters and roughly 20\% to per-step
wall-clock. Under teacher forcing the decoder achieves perfect
reconstruction on $76/94$ training games and a median per-position
match of $1.000$ (mean $0.906$, min $0.276$); the imperfect mean is
dominated by a small tail of long, sprite-rich games that exhaust
the decoder's positional budget. Greedy autoregressive decoding from
random Gaussian-fit slots ($N = 25$) yields $15/25$ engine-loadable
PuzzleScript programs (60\%); 9-point linear interpolations between
two endpoint games (\textsc{Ebony\_\&\_Ivory} $\rightarrow$
\textsc{rigidfail1}) yield $9/9$ engine-loadable programs along the
line (100\%). The generations contain plausible push rules of the
form \verb"[ > Obj2 | Obj4 ] -> [ > Obj2 | > Obj4 ]",
\texttt{late}-prefixed rules, and per-object $5{\times}5$ pixel
sprites with palettes consistent across the legend, the collision
layers, and the rule body.

\paragraph{Inverse-fitting unobserved games from transitions.}
\label{sec:results-inverse-fit}
To test whether an unobserved game's latent program can be recovered
from observed transitions, we freeze the trained NCA $\theta$ and
decoder $\psi$ and optimise the slot
$z \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d_{\text{slot}}}$ via 2{,}000 Adam steps
at $\text{lr} = 5 \times 10^{-3}$ on the same per-cell next-state
BCE used for training (\S\ref{sec:method-inverse}), holding model
parameters fixed. The held-out target games are drawn from the
deduped pool and verified disjoint from training by name and by
token-hash.

The optimisation drives BCE down two-to-three orders of magnitude on
every target, demonstrating that the frozen NCA can fit OOD dynamics
through the latent variable; the recovered slots land near
\emph{mechanically} related training games rather than arbitrary
ones, suggesting the latent space carries dynamics-relevant
structure that nearest-neighbour retrieval can use as a prior.
