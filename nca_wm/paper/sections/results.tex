\subsection{Symbolic autoencoder}
\label{sec:results-autoencoder}

We train the joint encoder + NCA + slot-token decoder
(\S\ref{sec:method}) on the 94-game broad-corpus preset
$\textsc{scaling\_gallery\_v3}$ for 150k updates with
$\beta = 1.0$ on the decoder loss. The decoder converges to per-token
cross-entropy $\approx 2 \times 10^{-9}$ within the first 5k updates
and holds there throughout. The world-model losses track the
encoder-only ablation ($\beta = 0$) within noise: final state-loss
$0.0049$ vs.\ $0.0049$, final change-error $0.013$ vs.\ $0.015$. The
decoder constraint is therefore essentially \emph{free} on prediction
quality at this scale, adding 1.18M parameters on top of the 9.89M
world-model parameters and roughly 20\% to per-step wall-clock.

\paragraph{Token reconstruction.}
Under teacher forcing the decoder achieves perfect reconstruction
($94/94$ games at $1.000$ per-position match). Slots therefore retain
all information needed to recover the full symbolic specification.

\paragraph{Held-out latent geometry.}
We encode a 30-game held-out set (disjoint from training by name and
by token-hash) through the trained encoder and measure each held-out
game's 1-NN cosine distance to the nearest training-game slot. With
$\beta = 1.0$ the median 1-NN distance falls from $0.215$ to $0.153$
(29\% tighter) versus the matched encoder-only run, holding all other
hyper-parameters fixed. The decoder objective appears to organise the
slot space so that mechanically-similar games sit closer.

\paragraph{Autoregressive sampling.}
Greedy autoregressive decoding from random Gaussian-fit slots
($N = 16$) and from 9-point linear interpolations between two
endpoint games yields $0/25$ JS-engine-loadable PuzzleScript
programs at the current scale. The decoded text is
\emph{structurally} valid --- push rules of the form
\verb"[ > Obj2 | Obj4 ] -> [ > Obj2 | > Obj4 ]", ellipsis rules
\verb"[ > ... | Obj1 ] -> [...]", and \texttt{late}-prefixed rules
all appear --- but the JS engine fails downstream because the
generated sequence emits dozens of redundant win-conditions instead
of stopping after one and references legend groups (\verb"Grp1",
\verb"Grp2") whose corresponding \texttt{GROUP\_OR} structure tokens
never appear in the autoregressive stream. This is a textbook
teacher-forcing-vs-autoregressive gap: the decoder has learned the
conditional next-token distribution but not a generation procedure
that respects document structure end-to-end. Standard remedies
(explicit \texttt{EOS}, scheduled sampling, structure-aware
constrained decoding) should close this; we treat the gap as future
work.

from observed transitions, we freeze the

parameters fixed. The held-out target games are drawn from the deduped

Two findings: (i) the optimisation drives BCE down two-to-three orders

The synthetic-multi-grid setting is the headline comparison: per-state
memorization is unavailable (4 widths $\times$ 256 synthetic levels
per width $=$ 1024 distinct levels share one rule set), so the model
must \emph{learn the rule that maps state plus action to the next
state}. Three observations: \textbf{(i)} the NCA with shared weights
again wins on BFS rollout ($4.37\% \pm 2.02\%$) at the fewest
parameters (3.07M), beating the matched-depth CNN
($5.56\% \pm 3.12\%$) and the per-step NCA
($5.95\% \pm 3.50\%$); the $2.8\times$ parameter advantage over
NCA-per-step suggests the shared-weights inductive bias is doing real
work, not just compressing capacity; \textbf{(ii)} U-Net
($8.33\% \pm 0.97\%$) and ViT ($12.70\% \pm 1.12\%$) underperform
here as well, with ViT also failing to fit the train loss
($0.084 \pm 0.002$ vs.\ $0.069 \pm 0.003$ for the other four);
\textbf{(iii)} the seed-to-seed variance on this task is large
($\sigma > 2\%$ on BFS error for all NCA / CNN variants), reflecting
the multiple-basin optimization landscape we previously characterized
on the closely related \textsc{varislide} task (F8); the large gap
to U-Net and ViT is robust to that variance.

\paragraph{(F1) Pool features are load-bearing.}
A bare $3{\times}3$ convolution can only propagate one cell of context
per NCA step. Several PuzzleScript rule patterns reference cells
arbitrarily far apart along an axis or anywhere on the level
(\texttt{[X | ... | Y]}, \texttt{[X][Y]}). Without pool features the
NCA needs $\Omega(\max(H,W))$ steps just to surface non-local
information; with axis-cummax + global pool one step suffices.
Across the 19-game \textsc{scaling\_large} preset and a controlled
\textsc{collapse} no-pool ablation, turning pool off costs both
convergence speed and final loss, with the no-pool model collapsing
into the identity-prediction minimum even at $4{\times}$ the depth.

\paragraph{(F2) Shared NCA-body weights are the right inductive bias.}
The PuzzleScript engine has two natural levels of iteration: an
inner ordered list of rules applied once each per tick, and an outer
\texttt{again} loop that re-runs the rule list until the state stops
changing. We factor $T = n_\ell \cdot n_r$ accordingly. On a single-game
depth sweep ($T \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$), shared $T{=}8$ reaches
$5.5{\times}$ lower train loss with $7{\times}$ fewer parameters than
the per-step body at the same depth; shared $T{=}2$ at 14$\times$
fewer parameters than the deepest per-step variant matches it on
oracle-action rollouts. Hierarchical $n_r > 1$ sits between these
extremes.

\paragraph{(F3) Train loss decouples from autoregressive rollout error.}
\label{sec:results-F3}
A model with the lowest train (per-step) loss is not the model with
the lowest autoregressive rollout error. Concretely, in the
over-capacity regime we observe per-step models with
$5{\times}$ lower train loss but $3{-}4{\times}$ higher 50-step
rollout error. The mechanism is small but systematic per-step errors
compounding destructively under autoregression. Reporting only
best-loss hides the rollout-drift cost of going deeper or wider.
Appendix~\ref{app:per-game-arch} reports a sharpened version of this
finding on a per-game $\times$ per-architecture grid: on
\textsc{Travelling\_salesman} all four buckets converge to within
$1\%$ of the same train loss but split $0\% / 0\% / 26.9\% / 45.1\%$
on L0 BFS cell-error.

\label{sec:results-pool}

Three observations:
\textbf{(i)} \emph{global-pool} is the single most important pool
feature. Removing it alone is as bad as removing all three pool
flavours combined ($+145\%$ relative BFS error in both cases),
indicating that grid-wide presence-of-X signals are doing all the
non-local work on this task; \textbf{(ii)} \emph{axis-cummax} ---
the directional prefix-max designed for axis-aligned ``rule fires
once X is in the same row/column as Y'' patterns --- contributes
nothing measurable on \textsc{sokoban\_basic} ($+0\%$). Sokoban
chained-pushing rules look like
\texttt{[ Crate | Crate ] -> [\,| Crate Crate\,]}, which depends
on the immediate $3{\times}3$ neighbourhood, not a directional axial
lookup. We expect the cummax feature to recover its load-bearing
role on games whose rule sets do reference far-apart same-axis cells
(e.g.\ \textsc{collapse}, \textsc{varislide});
\textbf{(iii)} \emph{input-skip} contributes a modest $+36\%$ but
with the highest variance across configurations
($\sigma = 4.24\%$), suggesting it is a stability mechanism rather
than an information-carrying feature: when training lands in a good
basin it isn't strictly needed, but it cuts the bad-basin tail.
Train loss is essentially identical ($0.098 \pm 0.004$) across all
six configurations despite a $2.5\times$ swing in BFS rollout
error --- yet another instance of F3 (\S\ref{sec:results-F3}).

\subsection{Adaptive halting}
\label{sec:results-halting}

We compare four halting modes and a fixed-depth baseline on a custom
$1$-rule \textsc{varislide} game in which a single \texttt{again}-driven
slide rule pushes the player up to $d \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16\}$
cells per action across grid widths $\{6, 8, 10, 12, 16\}$. Modes:
\emph{ponder} (PonderNet ponder loss with learned halt head + KL
prior), \emph{uniform} (per-step loss equally weighted across all
$T$ steps), \emph{argmax-ST} (straight-through estimator on
$\arg\max_k p_k$), and \emph{convergence-ST} (halt at inference when
consecutive predictions stop changing, no learned head).

\papertodo{Add halting figure: per-step error trajectory under each
mode plus per-instance halt distribution as a function of slide
distance.}

Across these modes we observe two phenomena. First, on tasks where
the dynamics fit cleanly in $\sim$1--2 NCA steps (small-grid varislide,
\textsc{collapse}), all halting modes converge near the dynamics-floor
within their effective depth and drift past it; convergence-ST
recovers near-oracle performance via inference-time stopping.
Second, on the multi-grid synthetic regime where memorization is
unavailable, \emph{none} of the halting modes reliably learn the
underlying rule (\S\ref{sec:results-ceiling}); the halt head at best
degenerates to halt-at-$k{=}1$ + identity prediction.

\subsection{Multi-grid synthetic data ceiling}
\label{sec:results-ceiling}

When we move from a single authored level to the multi-grid synthetic
data regime ($\{6,8,10,12,16\}$ widths $\times$ 64 levels each),
train change-error climbs by $1000{\times}$ ($5 \times 10^{-5}
\to 0.05$--$0.10$), confirming that the model can no longer
1-shot-memorize per-state outcomes. The model attempts to learn the
rule but plateaus: 50k updates at $d_h{=}256$ (5$\times$ more updates,
2$\times$ wider) does not move the needle. Multi-seed verification at
$T \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$ shows per-seed argmax variance dwarfs the
recipe signal and best-loss is essentially identical across all
configurations. We discuss candidate fixes in \S\ref{sec:discussion}.

scale. \textbf{(iv)} Per-game breakdowns and held-out cross-game
